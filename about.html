About

one trainer, one cup of coffee, one good plan.

I started training dogs in 2014 because I kept ending up with the ones nobody else wanted to walk. Reactive shepherds, anxious bullies, the screaming chihuahua at apartment 3B. After a decade, I've worked with more breeds than I can list and more behavioral oddities than I can remember by name.

New York, first

the old commute, roughly

Before any of this had a logo, I walked dogs in New York. Eight at a time, twelve hours a day, every weather. You learn to read a dog very fast when you're holding seven other leashes. New York teaches you to handle anything that happens on a sidewalk, because everything eventually does.

Portland came later. The dogs here are softer and weirder, and I mean both as compliments. Same job either way: teach the human to read the dog. Once you can read them, the rest is practice and the right plan.

How I work

a satisfied customer

Positive reinforcement, threshold training, classical conditioning. No shock collars, no prong collars, no "alpha" anything. The methods I use have names because they come from research, not from a guy on YouTube.

Every dog gets a real assessment. Every owner gets a real plan. We usually meet in person, in your space, where your dog already lives. Not near Portland? We do it over video, and it works better than you'd think. Sessions run 75 or 90 minutes. We start with what's hardest. We finish with something you can do tomorrow.

I take a small number of new clients each month. If you'd like to be one, the consult is free and there's no contract.
