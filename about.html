About

one trainer, one cup of coffee, one good plan.

I started training dogs in 2014 because I kept ending up with the ones nobody else wanted to walk. Reactive shepherds, anxious bullies, the screaming chihuahua at apartment 3B. After a decade, I've worked with more breeds than I can list and more behavioral oddities than I can remember by name.

I work in your home, in your neighborhood, with the dog you actually live with, not a clean version of them in a training facility. Most of what we'll do is teach the human to read the dog. Once you can read them, the rest is a question of practice and the right plan.

How I work

Positive reinforcement, threshold training, classical conditioning. No shock collars, no prong collars, no "alpha" anything. The methods I use have names because they come from research, not vibes.

Every dog gets a real assessment. Every owner gets a real plan. We meet in person, in your space, where your dog already lives. Sessions are 75 minutes. We start with what's hardest. We finish with something you can do tomorrow.

Two coasts of dogs

I started walking dogs full-time in New York around 2014. Eight dogs, twelve hours a day, every weather. New York teaches you to handle anything on a sidewalk. I moved to Portland a few years back, and the dogs here are softer and weirder and just as much fun to work with.

I take a small number of new clients each month. If you'd like to be one, the consult is free and there's no contract.
